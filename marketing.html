Reminders that understand you

A calendar that shows what you actually did, checkboxes, and a score for the things you finish — with an assistant that runs on your device, not on somebody's server.

Just say it

  • "Remind me to call the dentist on Friday at 3" Friday, 3pm, filed under Health — without you choosing any of it
  • "Take the pills every day at 9am" A daily rule, not ninety copies of the same row
  • "Pay the rent on the 1st" The next 1st, repeating monthly if you say so, filed under Money
  • "Push it back an hour" It knows which one you mean, because you were just talking about it
  • "What's overdue?" A list you can tick off without leaving the answer
  • "How am I doing?" Points, level, streak and last week — every figure the app's own

Why it is different

Nothing leaves the device

The language model is inside the app. There is no account, no sign-in and no network code — put the phone in Airplane Mode and every word of it still works, because nothing was ever going anywhere. Most "AI" apps are a text box in front of somebody else's server. This one cannot be.

The app does the understanding

"Next Friday", "the 5th", "every other Monday", "end of the month" — all worked out by the app itself, exactly, instantly, and the same way every time. Ask a small language model what those mean and it will answer confidently and differently each time.

It tells you what it did

Every date and every number on screen is the app's own. The model adds one sentence underneath, and if that sentence contains a figure it is thrown away before you see it. A reminder app that confidently tells you the wrong day is worse than one that says nothing.

A calendar that looks backwards too

The past is half the point

Most calendars only show what is coming. This one shows what happened: a cleared day, a half-done day and a missed day each look different — by shape rather than colour, so it works if you cannot tell them apart. Tap any day, in either direction, and see exactly what was on it.

Today is never an accusation

Today is ringed, not marked missed, until the day is actually over. An app that writes you off at nine in the morning is one you stop opening.

Late still counts

Tick something off two days late and it is credited to the day it belonged to, which is where you will look for it. A repeating reminder has many days and only the right one is marked.

A score you can predict

Points for finishing, more for finishing on time

Five points on low priority, ten on normal, fifteen on high, and five more for getting there before it was due. Un-tick something and the points come back off. Nothing is ever recalculated behind your back.

Streaks that are fair

Clear everything due on a day and the day counts. Days in a row are your streak — and a day with nothing due never breaks one. It is not a failure to have a free Sunday, and an app that punishes one is teaching you to invent busywork.

The rules are on the screen

Written out in plain words, in the same terms the app uses to compute them. A score nobody can predict is a score nobody trusts.

The rest of it

Repeats that match real life

Once, daily, weekly on the days you pick, monthly, yearly — or every other week, or every third day. All-day or at a set time. A heads-up notification from five minutes to a day before, and an optional morning plan and evening review.

Six lists, filled in for you

Personal, Work, Health, Home, Study and Money. "Pay the rent" goes to Money, "take the pills" to Health, "put the bins out" to Home — without you choosing, and changeable in one tap when the app guesses wrong.

An iPad app, not a big phone app

A sidebar that is always there, and two columns: the month beside the day you are reading, your list beside your score, the filters beside the results. Drawn for the space it has rather than stretched to fill it.

Two themes, one object

Black with light gold, white with dark gold, with a polished finish that catches the light rather than a flat wash that does not. The icon is one checkbox cut down the middle — silver on one side, gold on the other, each half wearing the other's metal, with the tick crossing the seam and changing as it goes.

It tells you at a civilised hour

Say "remind me to water the plants tomorrow" and you are told at nine in the morning, not at midnight. A day starts at midnight; being told about it does not. The hour is yours to change, and the editor prints it where you make the choice rather than keeping it as a surprise.

Readable at your size

Text follows your Text Size setting throughout, and the layout holds together at every step of it. Every control is named for VoiceOver, including the checkbox — which is a separate control from the row, so "open this" and "tick this off" are never the same gesture.

What it will never do

The short list

No account. No sign-up. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifiers. No network permission and no network code. Nothing you type is stored anywhere but on your device — and a second copy of your list is kept there too, so a damaged file does not cost you your history.
